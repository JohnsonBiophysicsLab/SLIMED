# Per-vertex boundary conditions (`boundaryType = Mixed`)

**Status:** implemented, tested (`tests/test_mixed_boundary.cpp`)
**Base:** `feature/monte-carlo-edge-flip @ 8e1a604`
**Scope:** a boundary mode in which every vertex carries its own boundary
condition -- free, fixed, or a periodic image of another vertex -- so that one
mesh can be periodic along one axis and clamped or open along another, and a
mesh read from a file needs no grid at all. The three global modes (`Fixed`,
`Periodic`, `Free`) are untouched; the shipped periodic workload is
byte-identical before and after this change (section 7).

## 1. Why

The three boundary modes the tree had were properties of the *run*, and every
rule that implemented them indexed the generated grid directly:
`determine_ghost_vertices_faces()` marked rows 0-2 and `nFaceY-2`..`nFaceY`
as ghosts, `get_relative_pt_periodic()` paired row 3 with row `nFaceY-3` by
arithmetic on `nFaceX`, the minimizer's boundary enforcement and the force
zeroing did the same sums again in two more places. That describes exactly
one object: a rectangular sheet with the same boundary on all four sides. It
cannot describe

- a sheet periodic along `x` and clamped along `y` (a strip held at two edges),
- a tube or bottleneck that is periodic along its axis and open at a neck,
- any mesh that was not generated by `set_vertices_faces_flat()`, because an
  imported mesh has `nFaceX = -1` and every grid rule silently does nothing.

Under `boundaryType = Mixed` the direction of the question is reversed. Each
vertex says what it is (`VertexType`), and the treatment follows from the
vertices: which of them are degrees of freedom, where the force accumulated on
an image goes, which faces carry energy. The grid is never consulted, so the
generated sheet and an imported mesh go through the same code.

## 2. The vocabulary

`VertexType` (`include/mesh/Vertex.hpp`):

| type | meaning | moves? | force? |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `Free` | an ordinary vertex, on an open edge included | yes | its own, plus that of its images |
| `Fixed` | clamped in space | no | zeroed |
| `Periodic` | an image of `reflectiveVertexIndex`, at that source plus `mirrorOffset` | with its source | folded onto its source, then zeroed |
| `Ghost` | scaffolding only (what the global modes write) | no | zeroed |

The integer values of the enum are unchanged (`Free = Real = 0`, `Fixed = 1`,
`Periodic = PeriodicReflectiveBoundary = 3`, `Ghost = 4`); the old names are
kept as aliases and the vertex-type output that `analysis/fluidity.py` reads
is the same file.

Two derived flags complete the picture:

- **`Face::isGhost`** -- the face is a *copy* of a physical face and carries
  no energy, area or volume. Under `Mixed` this is decided per face by
  `Mesh::mark_periodic_copy_faces()`, not by grid row.
- **`Vertex::isGhost`** -- the vertex is an image none of whose faces carries
  energy (or is typed `Ghost` outright). It is pinned in the surface solver
  and skipped by the step. An image that *does* corner an energy-carrying
  face -- the duplicate ring of a periodic sheet -- is not ghost: it is a
  control point of real patches, slaved to its source.

`Mesh::is_independent_vertex(v)` is the one predicate for "is this a
coordinate of the model": not ghost, not fixed, not an image.

## 3. What an image is

An image is not a coordinate. It sits at

```text
    x_img = x_src + t_img
```

with `t_img` a fixed translation recorded at setup from the initial positions
(`Mesh::resolve_periodic_images()`), and it is put back there after every
coordinate update (`Mesh::sync_periodic_images()` in the minimizer;
`SurfaceSolver::surface_to_mesh()` writes the image rows itself in the
dynamics). A chain of images -- an image whose source is itself an image -- is
flattened to the source at the end of the chain, and a chain that never reaches
a source is refused as a cycle.

Because the energy is a function of the sources alone, with the images tied
to them, the chain rule gives the force on a source as

```text
    F_src = -dE/dx_src (direct) + sum over images of -dE/dx_img
```

`Mesh::fold_forces_onto_periodic_sources()` adds every image's accumulated
force to its source and zeroes the image. That sum is the exact gradient of
the periodic energy, at the seam as well as in the interior; the
finite-difference check in
`MixedBoundaryTest.TheFoldedForceIsMinusTheGradientOfThePeriodicEnergy`
moves a source together with its images and compares. The global `Periodic`
mode discards the image forces instead (it zeroes its ghosts and leaves its
duplicates un-integrated), so the two rows nearest each seam carry forces
that are not the gradient of anything. That approximation is one of the
things this mode removes.

Two consequences of exactness that are easy to miss:

- **Copy faces are left out of every per-face term**, the reference-length
  regularization included. A copy face is the same physical triangle as the
  face it duplicates, and its corners are images or sources of that face's
  corners; charging it would count the seam faces twice and push the double
  count onto the sources through the folding. (The global modes keep their
  ghost faces in the regularization, as they always have; nothing there
  changes.) The device backends apply the same rule through
  `ForceKernelArgs::regularizationSkipsGhostFaces`.
- **The tether and the crease wall already excluded copies** through
  `Mesh::edge_carries_tether()` and the ghost-face tests; images are frozen for
  the flip move exactly as the global mode's duplicates are.

## 4. The surface solver

The dynamics integrates the Loop limit surface `S = M C` and solves back for
the control net. In `SurfaceSolver` (`include/dynamics/Surface_solver.hpp`)
an image gets no row: wherever a free vertex's mask reaches an image, the
source's unknown is substituted and the offset moves to the right-hand side,

```text
    N_v C_v + sum over neighbours u of C_src(u) = 2 N_v S_v - sum over neighbours of t_u .
```

What remains is `K = (D + A)/2` on the *wrapped* adjacency -- the adjacency of
the periodic sheet rather than of its finite tile -- which is symmetric
exactly when the image structure is consistent (if an image of `w` neighbours
`v`, an image of `v` neighbours `w`). `build()` checks that and refuses a mesh
whose images say otherwise, naming the pair. Fixed vertices are pinned
(known, moved to the right-hand side), images are written from their sources
after the solve, and the force map is the transpose of the same map --
correct provided the nodal force handed in has been folded, which
`Compute_Energy_And_Force()` guarantees.

The dense conversion cannot express any of this: it builds `M` as a matrix
with a row per vertex, and a row cannot say "this vertex is that one plus a
constant". `DynamicMesh::finish_dynamic_setup()` therefore requires
`surfaceSolver = iterative` under `Mixed`. The minimizer has no such
requirement.

## 5. The generated sheet

With `boundaryType = Mixed` the flat-sheet generator reads two more keys:

```text
boundaryType = Mixed
boundaryTypeX = Periodic       # Periodic | Free | Fixed
boundaryTypeY = Fixed
fixedBoundaryRings = 1         # rings of vertices a Fixed side clamps
surfaceSolver = iterative      # required by the dynamics under Mixed
```

- **Periodic** lays out the band the global mode lays out -- three rings of
  images and one ring of duplicates on each side, sources at grid indices
  `3 .. nFace-4` -- as `Periodic` vertices mirroring the vertex one period
  away. The copy faces are the same cells the global mode calls ghost, so
  every output lines up index for index with a global `Periodic` run of the
  same sheet (`MixedBoundaryTest.ThePeriodicSheetLaysOutTheSameBandAsTheGlobalMode`).
  A periodic axis needs at least 10 faces: the band and a period of at
  least four, since the one-ring of a face spans four rows.
- **Fixed** clamps the outermost `fixedBoundaryRings` rings of sources. One
  ring pins the control points on the edge and leaves the slope free; two
  rings pin the tangent as well. The outermost ring of faces carries no
  patch either way, because its one-ring is incomplete.
- **Free** leaves the edge open. The outermost ring of faces has no complete
  one-ring and so no limit surface; the edge vertices are degrees of freedom
  and move under the forces of the faces one ring in, whose control nets
  include them.

`Periodic`/`Periodic` is the global periodic sheet with the seam made exact,
and is the A/B for the seam physics. `Periodic`/`Fixed` is the strip held at
two edges. `Periodic`/`Free` is a strip with two open edges.

## 6. Mesh files

A run loads its mesh from files instead of generating the sheet when both are
named:

```text
meshVerticesFile = mesh_vertices.csv
meshFacesFile = mesh_faces.csv
```

The format (`include/io/io.hpp`, `read_mesh_vertices_faces_files()`):

```text
# vertices: one per line, indexed from 0 in file order
x, y, z                       a free vertex
x, y, z, type                 type: free | fixed | periodic | ghost (or the integer)
x, y, z, periodic, mirror     an image of vertex `mirror`

# faces: one triangle per line, consistently wound
v0, v1, v2
v0, v1, v2, copy              copy: 0 | real  or  1 | copy
```

Blank lines and `#` lines are skipped; a line with no comma is split on
whitespace; a trailing empty field (the legacy type writer ends every line
with a comma) is ignored. Errors name the file and line.

Every `Mixed` run writes its mesh in this format beside its other outputs --
`mesh_vertices.csv` / `mesh_faces.csv` from the minimizer,
`<prefix>mesh_vertices.csv` / `<prefix>mesh_faces.csv` from the dynamics --
through `export_mesh_to_vertices_faces()`, at full precision, so a mesh SLIMED
wrote loads back as the same mesh: types, mirrors, offsets and copy flags
included (`MixedBoundaryTest.ExportingAndImportingReproducesTheMesh`).
`data/example/mixed_sheet_vertices.csv` and `mixed_sheet_faces.csv` are one
such pair, a 60 nm sheet periodic along `x` and clamped along `y`.

Without a copy-flag column the copies are derived from the mirror map
(`Mesh::mark_periodic_copy_faces()`): faces whose corners map to the same
three sources are copies of one physical face, and the one with the most
source corners keeps the energy, the lowest index among equals. Which of two
faces astride a seam is kept is a convention; that every physical face is
charged exactly once is not, and it is tested
(`MixedBoundaryTest.CopiesDerivedFromTheMirrorMapTileThePeriodOnce`).

A file with any type other than `free` needs `boundaryType = Mixed`. The
global modes decide the boundary from grid position and would silently ignore
the column, so they refuse the file instead. A file without a type column
loads under every mode exactly as before.

### 6.1 Building a custom mesh

The requirements a hand-built or script-built mesh has to meet, in the order
setup checks them:

1. **Consistent winding.** Every shared edge is traversed in opposite
   directions by its two faces. The importer does not re-wind
   (`sort_vertices_on_faces()` would rebaseline the patches), and
   `validate_volume_constraint_topology()` is what reports an inconsistent
   mesh when a volume constraint is on.
2. **Mirrors name sources.** A `periodic` vertex names the vertex it is an
   image of; chains are allowed, cycles are not. The offset is read off the
   coordinates, so put the image where it belongs.
3. **A deep enough image band.** Every energy-carrying face needs a complete
   one-ring, which reaches two vertices out from the face, so a periodic seam
   needs images two rings beyond the last energy-carrying face and the
   duplicate ring in between -- the three-plus-one layout the generator uses.
   A face that touches the seam and has no complete one-ring is not an
   error: it becomes a boundary face with no patch and no energy, which is
   right for an open edge and wrong for a seam. The setup summary line
   (`[Mesh::apply_vertex_boundary_types] ... N carry energy, M are copies`)
   and the valence histogram say how many faces lost their patch.
4. **A consistent image structure.** If an image of `w` neighbours `v`, an
   image of `v` must neighbour `w`; the solver refuses the mesh otherwise,
   naming the pair. A proper periodic tiling has this automatically.
5. **One representative per physical face**, either through the flag column
   or by leaving it to the derivation above.

The tube-with-a-neck case: the tube is periodic along its axis (images at
both ends, three rings deep, and the duplicate ring), and the neck's rim
vertices are `free` with open fans. Around the circumference a tube is
closed, so nothing there is periodic in the file's sense -- the triangles
simply wrap.

## 7. What did not change

- The global modes run the code they always ran. The shipped periodic
  workload -- `membrane_dynamics` on a 100 nm `Periodic` sheet for 300 steps
  and `continuum_membrane` for 25 iterations -- produces byte-identical
  output files (all eight of the dynamics', all thirteen of the minimizer's)
  with the binaries built before and after this change.
- `Mesh::write_vertices_csv_with_type()` writes the same integers it always
  wrote; the new exporter is a separate pair of files.
- `read_data_from_csv<T>()` and the two-argument
  `Mesh::setup_from_vertices_faces()` are still there and still do what they
  did; the new five-argument overload is what the typed path uses.

## 8. Limits

- **The seam is solid.** Images are frozen for the flip move, so a fluid run
  on a `Mixed` periodic sheet keeps a band four vertices wide solid at each
  seam, as the global mode does. Mirroring an accepted flip onto the images
  is the extension that would make the seam fluid; it is not done here.
- **Pre-refinement is refused on a typed mesh.** Refinement puts a new vertex
  on every edge and would have to give each one on an image edge an image of
  its own. Refine before typing.
- **The dynamics needs the iterative solver** (section 4).
- **A mesh with images encloses no volume**, so a volume constraint on it is
  refused at setup, as it is for the global `Periodic` and `Free` modes. A
  `Mixed` mesh without images -- a closed surface with some vertices clamped
  -- is judged on its topology like any other.
- **Insertion patches and scaffolding** are indexed by face and by vertex
  and know nothing about images; a face carrying an insertion is excluded
  from flips as before, and a scaffold bond to an image vertex would pull on
  something that is not a coordinate. Bond scaffolds to sources.
